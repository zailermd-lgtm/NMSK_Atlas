NMSK Atlas

Six whole-body viewers in three kinds, kept apart: Base models are Z-Anatomy, the textbook body, with nothing fitted to a person. Imaging-based models are rebuilt from the Visible Human CT and cryosections, one real body each; where the scan has nothing, Z-Anatomy fills the gap and is badged as a fill. Combined models are the Z-Anatomy body moved onto one of those reconstructions. The green card in each row is the latest version of that model; artifacts whose title starts with OLD are superseded. Every viewer opens in its own tab and works on a phone, tablet and computer; two fingers zoom any picture toward the point between them, and a small 1:1 chip takes it back.

All viewers share one atlas frame: +X right · +Y superior · +Z anterior, millimetres, origin at the midpoint of the two hip-joint centres. The same point has the same coordinates in every viewer.
Since Q301 (2 Nov 2026) there is one control, and it never folds away: a bar across the bottom of the 3‑D view carrying Undo · Redo · Reset module · Reset model · ⓘ, each with its name printed under the icon, over one row of labelled groups — View · Layers · Imaging · Cut · Mark · Learn · Measure. One group opens at a time and the others' buttons are not on the screen at all; a group carries a dot while anything inside it is active. The groups adopt the engines' own buttons, moving the real nodes in with their ids and their listeners, so nothing exists twice and the old crowded bars empty themselves. Which actions combine is now stated and checked in a browser rather than assumed: cut and the radiographic styles read “+ they work together: the cut face is drawn in the style's own grey”, while an incompatible pair is disabled with its reason printed under it, not buried in a tooltip a phone never shows; and where one action simply takes precedence over another — a radiographic style pauses Q300's extra bone pass, because it sets the bone's brightness itself — that is said too, instead of disabling either. The model itself can now be drawn as a radiograph, a CT or an MRI, with one tap back to colour. Every number comes from the tissue table the pages already ship (33 classes, CT attenuation in Hounsfield units, MRI pools at 1.5 T, each value carrying its own verification status) and none is retyped in the viewer: the X‑ray is a true digitally reconstructed radiograph — alpha = 1 − e−µd, so ordinary alpha compositing over black accumulates Beer–Lambert's e−Σµd, order‑independently, with every closed shell crossed twice as a real beam crosses it — the CT is each tissue's own HU through a real window (MSK, soft tissue, bone), and the MRI is each tissue's own spin‑echo signal (T1, T2, PD fat‑sat). The right answers come out: cortical bone white and fat dark on CT, fat brightest on T1, free fluid brightest on T2, fat suppressed on PD fat‑sat. Measured in rendered pixels, not by eye: a style drops the picture's colour saturation from 57–108 to 0–2 and makes 100 % of its pixels pure grey, and the three styles sit tens of grey levels apart. What is convention rather than measurement is named in the ⓘ, including the path length per crossing (chosen by measuring where the contrast peaks) and the 15 of 33 classes whose CT value the data file itself marks unverified. All the page‑wide prose — licence, citations, conventions, the compatibility table — moved behind a small circled ⓘ at the side; the structure card keeps saying where its own geometry comes from. Plain fat is off when the page loads and one tap away, while still filling every cut face and cross‑section, because a section drawn without it reads as hollow. And beside the per‑system controls there is now one Depth control that peels the body inward in taps, with the layer name and number on the screen: skin → fat / superficial fascia → deep fascia → muscle → ligament → vessels and nerves → organs, leaving bone and cartilage. The fat step is the superficial fascia, by the structures' own Latin names — 17 of the 51 subcutaneous‑fat meshes are labelled Panniculus adiposus (tela subcutanea). What these pages do not carry is a separate membranous (Scarpa's) layer, and their own search data already said so; the ⓘ passes that on, and a test asserts the absence against the shipped pages rather than taking it on trust.
Since Q300 (1 Nov 2026) you can always get back out. The moment anything is hidden — an isolated structure, a tissue soloed, a preset that puts every tissue it does not name at zero, a side filter, a cut, a structure hidden from its own card — a chip appears at the bottom of the 3‑D view naming it, with its own × that undoes only that one, beside one Show everything button that ends them all; Esc does the same on a desktop, and every exit is a single Undo step. Nine separate ways of hiding something used to have nine exits in nine places, and nothing on screen said what was hidden. Beside the module reset there is now a reset of the model: pose, camera, visibility, isolation, opacity, marks, cuts, cross‑sections, slices, measurements and labels back to how the page loaded, in one tap, without leaving the module, undoable. And the skeleton is visible again in x‑ray and in fade: the bone is drawn once more over the soft tissue with the depth test off, so it no longer loses a multiplicative fight against however many translucent layers lie in front of it, with a three‑step Bone: Off · Clear · Strong control that appears only in those modes. Measured in rendered pixels, not by eye.
Since Q299 (1 Nov 2026) nothing on any page stands outside the body any more, except where being outside is the anatomically correct answer. A containment test was built that actually works on these pages — a voxelised body mask from each page's own skin and subcutaneous tissue, validated against 26 structures that are inside by construction, with its own error band measured (p95 1.3 mm) — because the ray-parity test Q297 tried is meaningless against ~250 open skin patches. It found that on Base · Female the external pudendal veins and arteries stood up to 21.7 mm in front of the skin of the mons pubis, with the genitofemoral nerves at 11.5 mm and the HuBMAP HRA breast objects at 7–24 mm. Base · Male is clean, which is the whole explanation: on the male page those vessels end inside the external genital skin the female pages no longer carry. The fix is Q250's own skin guard re-run against a mask it can trust — the guard had logged 1,057 vertices as “only counted” because it could not tell inside from outside — and the four vessels went to 0.00 mm with no flipped triangle. Bone is never moved and the ocular surface is exempt, both stated. The pelvis itself was re-measured against 47 women segmented from CT and measured with the same geometric rules: her own pelvis really is wide (+2.2 SD), which is her and not a defect, but three fit defects are now stated on the cards — Combined · Female's femoral heads sit 13 mm too far apart because Q250 never re-seated them, its sciatic notch is 16° too narrow, and Base · Female's ischium is 8 mm too wide for her. And the cross-section: a section too thin for the raster, or an open sheet that fails the parity test, is now drawn as a band of a minimum drawn width (1/500 of the section, capped at 2.5 mm, the true millimetres kept on record) — measured over 210 axial levels per page, invisible vessel sections fell from 527–608 per Z page to 0–5 and fascia from 1,754–1,918 to 44–105. The peroneal nerve the owner could not find in a cross-section of the male model is a content gap, not a drawing one: the Imaging pages carry no fibular nerve at all (12 lower-limb nerves against 81 on Combined · Male), which is now written down and put to the owner as a decision.
Since Q298 (1 Nov 2026) the search on every viewer finds a structure by any name a clinician would use. The owner looked for the iliotibial tract as “ITB” and as “iliotibial band” and got nothing: each viewer was matching the query as one contiguous substring of one field, so an abbreviation could never match, a two-word query failed unless those words were next to each other in that order (“iliotibial band” missed “Iliotibial (IT) tract/band”, which contains both), the Latin name and the group were never searched at all, and one typo returned nothing. Now ITB, IT band, iliotibial band, tractus iliotibialis and הרצועה האיליוטיביאלית all reach it, and the row says which name matched — Iliotibial tract (ITB) — so you learn the canonical name. The layer is a real data file of published abbreviations, second names, Latin forms, Hebrew names and the eponyms this project cites, resolved against each viewer's own structure list at build time; ambiguous abbreviations (GM, TA) offer every expansion and name each one, and a name we cite but ship no mesh for (Colles’ and Scarpa’s fascia, Gerdy’s tubercle, the pectineal ligament of Cooper) says so instead of returning nothing. Marking several structures is now one action: Mark all N takes every match of a search in a single undo step, Range takes a run of rows, and On model turns on marking by tapping the body. And after you pick one structure, three one-tap expansions add to the marked set: Adjacent — measured on that viewer’s own meshes, every surface rasterised into a shared 2 mm voxel grid, neighbours within 3.46 mm (10,190 to 58,015 neighbour pairs per viewer); Related — the same muscle’s other heads, its tendon, bursae, nerve supply, the artery that supplies it, the bones it attaches to, the joints it crosses and its antagonists, from this project’s own cited data, with the four rule-based kinds labelled inferred; and Functional group — the named textbook groups, the groups derived from the cited action data, and extras such as the tensors of the iliotibial tract (tensor fasciae latae and gluteus maximus). Searching a group name (“hamstrings”, “rotator cuff”, “quads”, “calf”) offers the group itself as one chip.
Since Q297 (1 Nov 2026) every mesh on every viewer has been traced to its source and classified: 803 measured from that subject's own imaging, 12,899 a real mesh from another body registered onto the page, 1,451 rule-based with a citation, and 0 that cannot be accounted for. Two could not, and both are gone. The perineal surface closure on Base · Female and Combined · Female was a surface this project generated — a smooth sheet fitted over the hole left when Z-Anatomy's male-only urogenital skin was removed for a female body — with no anatomical source and no citation for its shape (8.3 cm³ and 16.4 cm³), and on Base · Female it was drawn at load like ordinary skin. It has been removed and the perineum is now shown open: no source we hold contains female perineal skin, and an honest hole beats invented tissue. The perineal muscles, the perineal body, the vagina and the clitoris stay — they are real segmentations of another woman (Chinese Visible Human, Wu et al. 2015, CC BY 4.0) and say so on their cards. The round also found 68 rule-built brachial-plexus meshes whose cards read “Measured on this body” and 16 nerve cords whose cards claimed Z-Anatomy geometry; both are corrected, and every rule-based structure now has a visible badge and a recorded volume. The breasts were re-measured and are not a broken mesh — the shape is her supine cadaver CT, and on Base · Female the bone warp doubles the left/right difference; the fingertips were re-measured and the cause is that Z-Anatomy ships no nail, nail bed or digital pulp at all. Both are stated on the cards rather than sculpted away.
Since Q296 (31 Oct 2026) the four Z-Anatomy viewers (Base and Combined) carry four inner-ear structures no model here had before: the bony labyrinth (cochlea, vestibule and the three semicircular canals as one cavity, 0.17-0.20 cm³), the osseous spiral lamina, the round window and the stapedius muscle, on both sides. They are real meshes imported from the SPL Inner Ear Atlas of the Open Anatomy Project (flat-panel CT of a human temporal bone, about 140 µm; Bartling, Jakab and Kikinis, German Cancer Research Center and the Surgical Planning Laboratory, Brigham and Women's Hospital; 3D Slicer License Part B, research use only), placed by registering that atlas's own malleus, incus and stapes onto each page's own three ossicles (residual 0.20 mm median, 0.57 mm p95). Each card says so, names the citation and the licence, and states that the mesh is a modified version of the atlas model (rescaled, decimated, and mirrored on the left, because the atlas ear is a right ear). The two imaging-based viewers are unchanged: neither her CT nor his cryosections resolve the ossicles, so there is nothing there to register onto.
Since Q295 (29 Oct 2026) every viewer has a Fat system: subcutaneous fat, deep fat (intermuscular, visceral / retroperitoneal, ischioanal, orbital, buccal, popliteal, axillary) and the remaining unlabelled soft tissue, 51 meshes per body, measured on the Visible Human female's CT (fat HU) and the male's colour cryosections in the space no other structure covers (the Base pages carry them by a registration onto the Z body). Faded in 3-D at load, it fills the cut face, so a cross-section is no longer mostly empty (void inside the skin 26-86 % before, 8-40 % after, per region).
Since Q294 (29 Oct 2026) every viewer has Undo / Redo (60 steps) and a Reset of the current module, a one-line summary of what is marked and how it is shown, slices of one part (a finger, the forearm) with thickness, interval and a display-only spread, full names behind abbreviations, and distance / angle / Cobb measuring on the model and the image panes; in the ultrasound, thin sheets keep their true thickness at every zoom, the probe is dragged on the skin with a rotate / tilt pad, each probe type has its own shape, and deltoid, pectoralis major, trapezius and rectus femoris have fibres per bundle.
Since Q293 (29 Oct 2026) every viewer has the reflected (indirect) head of rectus femoris, built by stated rules from cadaveric dimensions and badged rule-based; the Combined hands are re-articulated on their own bones (the phalanges were seated apart); and the Learn simulation moves every joint of a two-joint muscle in one phase (rectus femoris flexes the hip while it extends the knee).
