NMSK Atlas

Six whole-body viewers in three kinds, kept apart: Base models are Z-Anatomy, the textbook body, with nothing fitted to a person. Imaging-based models are rebuilt from the Visible Human CT and cryosections, one real body each; where the scan has nothing, Z-Anatomy fills the gap and is badged as a fill. Combined models are the Z-Anatomy body moved onto one of those reconstructions. The green card in each row is the latest version of that model; artifacts whose title starts with OLD are superseded. Every viewer opens in its own tab and works on a phone, tablet and computer; two fingers zoom any picture toward the point between them, and a small 1:1 chip takes it back.

Since Q307 (3 Nov 2026) every nerve on every page was checked for where it actually is, after the owner reported the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve as out of place. The answer, measured on the pages' own geometry: that nerve is in the right compartment, on the right side, at the right depth and attached to the right parent — it runs +9 to +18 mm radial of the radius' own axis with the ulna's axis at −22 to −29, a median 0.95–2.08 mm from the brachioradialis surface, and it meets the musculocutaneous nerve at 0.02–1.41 mm on the Z pages — but only its proximal two thirds exists: it stops 53.8–73.5 mm short of the wrist and has no anterior and no posterior branch on any page. And on the two Imaging‑based pages it did not exist at all, along with 95 other nerves the Combined page of the same body carries, 33 of them in the upper limb. Q307 carries those across with Q304's own instrument: 57 onto Imaging · Male and 60 onto Imaging · Female. A defect in Q304's own work was found by the audit and fixed: its selector skipped any id the page already carried, so Imaging · Male's right tibial nerve was a 40 mm mirrored stub while its left was the full 524 mm nerve — under the same name — and the right plantar nerves hung 436.6 mm from their own parent; Imaging · Female had it on the left for both the tibial and the femoral nerve, with its plantar nerves 370.4 mm adrift. All three are now the full nerve and every branch touches its parent (0.00–0.71 mm, measured in the browser). Only a mirror of the other side could be replaced, so nothing traced on the body's own imaging was lost. What is still wrong is written on the cards, not sculpted away: the missing distal third and the missing branches, with both cadaveric sources quoted (Khadanovich 2023, 93 forearms: the nerve divides in 81.7 % of them; Qawasmi 2024, 28 fresh limbs: it is deep to the forearm superficial fascia in all of them, which is why this nerve not lying in the subcutaneous fat is correct and not a defect). 57 cutaneous nerves across the six pages carry their measured depth inside a muscle belly on their own cards — worst 17.8 mm in the gastrocnemius — and none was moved, because re‑routing a nerve needs a source and this round has none for it. One finding this round nearly reported and then killed with a measurement: an uncalibrated test said 48–65 % of the owner's nerve was buried inside the brachioradialis. The voxel mask is inflated by about one voxel (116.7 cm³ against the mesh's own 93.8), and after calibration the true penetration is 0.4–1.3 mm: the nerve is pressed against the muscle, exactly as the literature says it should be.
Since Q305 (3 Nov 2026) you can move the boxes on the screen to wherever you want them. Every box that floats over the model — the control bar (its open group travels with it), the “what is hidden” bar, the information drawer, the cut / cross‑section panel, the structure card, the side panel, the top row of buttons, the measurement bar, the part‑slices panel, the structure‑expansion strip, the labels panel and legend — carries a small handle of six dots at one corner. Drag the handle and the box goes where you put it; it is still there next time you open that page in that browser. The box's body is never a drag, and that is the point: the model is dragged to turn it, a pinch zooms any picture and two fingers pan it, the ultrasound probe is dragged over the skin, and a long press on a list row picks a range — a box that moved when its body was dragged would take one of those away. The one documented precedent in any comparable tool is 3D Slicer, whose module panel is undocked “by left‑clicking and dragging the panel chrome”, i.e. its title bar; the title bars of these boxes are full of buttons, so the handle is its own 44 px target instead. On a phone the boxes dock rather than float, and that was measured: at 390×844 the control bar has −8 px of sideways freedom and the side panel −24 px, so an axis with less than one thumb (48 px) of travel is given a few slots — three bands for a wide box, four corners for a small one — and a drop snaps to the nearest. It cannot take a control away from you: after every drop, elementFromPoint at the centre of Undo, Redo, both resets, the circled i, Show everything and the control bar's own buttons checks that each is still reachable, and a drop that would cover one is refused — the box goes back and one line says which control it was. Restore layout (in View on the control bar, and in the circled i) puts every box back in one tap, as a single Undo step; Reset model does it too, because the layout is part of what the page loaded in; and on a keyboard the arrow keys move the box whose handle has focus, with Home putting that one back. What is deliberately not movable: the zoom chip (it reports the zoom of the picture it sits on, so where it is is what it means), a four‑pane label (it names its own quadrant), the imaging and ultrasound views (those are panes the layout sizes, not boxes), and on the Imaging pages the structure list and card, which are columns of the page's grid — a moved column would leave an empty strip and cover the model it had just made room for. Accessibility, stated plainly: WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.7 asks that anything done by dragging have another way without dragging — the arrow keys on the focused handle, Home on that one box and the one‑tap Restore layout are provided, and WCAG evaluates keyboard and pointer independently — but there is no pointer‑only “tap the handle, then tap the destination”, so full coverage of that SC is not claimed; it is written up as a decision for the owner instead of asserted. And one inherited defect was fixed on the way: in the control bar's View group the Base and Combined pages' bottom pill row was adopted into the sheet but stayed pinned to the bottom of the screen, painting over every row below it — Restore layout included. The cause was CSS specificity, not layout (an id outranks the two classes the group styles its adopted rows with), and one declaration scoped to the sheet fixes it, leaving the pill row in its own place at the bottom of the screen untouched.
Since Q304 (3 Nov 2026) the two Imaging‑based pages carry the lower‑limb nerves they were missing. They are not his imaging, and every card says so. Each card carries a Method row, a ±N mm error band, a badge that opens “NOT SEGMENTED FROM THIS SUBJECT’S OWN IMAGING”, and the source page’s own badge quoted verbatim so the original fit’s error is not lost; the source line reads “Filled from Z‑Anatomy — not measured here”. Nothing added here can print “Measured on this body”, and the provenance audit after the change still reports 0 such cards and 0 structures with no traceable source. And the sciatic nerve’s calibre is now checked against a real measurement: SPARC dataset 692 (10.26275/1jdc‑qiiv, CC BY 4.0 — an embalmed 41‑year‑old male cadaver’s own sciatic nerves at 11.4 µm), at that dataset’s own bone‑referenced levels. Calibre only, never shape: those samples were excised and laid straight, so they carry no in‑body trajectory. Ours is within about a quarter of the measured diameter in the mid and distal thigh and 1.4–2.3× too thick proximally. No geometry was changed — a fixed, stained, excised nerve is a lower bound on a living one, and it is one subject — so the difference is written on the sciatic nerve’s card on all six pages instead, with the DOI.
Since Q303 (2 Nov 2026) the imaging goes where you already pointed. Entering CT / MR, the probe (the ultrasound add‑on, which the controls call simply the probe because the public layer may not name the private tools), a cut, the cross‑sections or one of the radiographic styles now centres that mode on what you already chose — the marked structures (their combined bounding box), else the structure whose card is open, else the result you picked out of a search — and with none of those, nothing moves at all. The CT / MR slice point becomes the centre and every pane's own in‑plane centre is released so it follows it (the slice level and the in‑plane position in one move); the probe is seated on the skin where a ray from the camera through the centre meets it; the cut plane keeps the orientation you chose and only slides along its own normal; the cross‑section stack is centred on that same plane; a radiographic style frames the camera. A line says what it centred on and why, it is a single Undo step, and Follow what I chose (in View, on by default) switches it off for good if you would rather it never moved. The pattern is 3D Slicer's “Jump slices — centered”, which that module offers as an explicit choice against offset, so it is a choice here too. Four views turns the 3‑D view into four panes — Anterior · Lateral · Superior · 3‑D — each with its own camera: Slicer's Four‑Up set, and one model seen four ways, not four models, because selection, marking, hiding, the layers, the peel, the style and the cut are the engine's single state and the panes are four viewports over that one scene. The pane you touch owns the camera; one tap on a pane's square shows only that pane, and restore brings the four back. It stays 2×2 on a phone, and that was decided by measuring — 195 × 422 px per pane, every square hit‑testable, a real touch picks under that pane's own camera, no horizontal scroll — while the overlays that are drawn for one camera (labels, the axis gnomon, the scale bar, the cut outline, the part markers, the measuring chips) stand down until you expand a pane. Facing me turns the selected structure to face you by one rule that becomes two: the camera looks along the structure's own axis of least spread, which for a flat structure is its surface normal and for a tube lies across the shaft, so the long axis runs across the screen at full length — measurably the largest view of it there is, and the camera lands within 2° of that axis. Save this view remembers the camera under a name and brings it back in one tap (up to eight, in this browser only, camera and pane layout and nothing else); the standard front / side / back views are unchanged. And the nerves are visible again, the small ones included. Why they were not was measured first: the pages' own calibre table gives every nerve a diameter measured off its mesh, the median is 1.0 mm on the four Z pages, and a whole‑body view is about 1.9 mm per pixel — so most nerves were drawn well under one pixel wide, which no colour or opacity can rescue. Nerves: Off · Clear · Strong draws one more pass over the nerves' own vertices, after everything and on the camera‑facing half of the model, each vertex a round dot at least the step's width across — Q299's minimum drawn width and Q300's extra bone pass, applied to nerves. Which structures it covers is the page's own answer: every structure its calibre table records as a nerve — 439 of the 552 nerve‑category meshes on a Z page — the trunks and every named branch; the spinal dura, the cord, its tracts and its nuclei are not peripheral nerves and are left exactly as they are, and the control says how many that is. A nerve already wider than the minimum is not touched, no mesh is changed, the true calibre stays the record on the card and in the legend, and the emphasis is labelled a display aid. It switches the nerve system on rather than fighting it, is clipped by the cut, and carries the motor‑point markers with it; in a greyscale radiographic view it is marked incompatible and says what to use instead. Where a page ships no such nerve at all — the two Imaging pages carry no fibular (peroneal) nerve — the control says so, because emphasis cannot show a nerve that is not there.
Since Q301 (2 Nov 2026) there is one control, and it never folds away: a bar across the bottom of the 3‑D view carrying Undo · Redo · Reset module · Reset model · ⓘ, each with its name printed under the icon, over one row of labelled groups — View · Layers · Imaging · Cut · Mark · Learn · Measure. One group opens at a time and the others' buttons are not on the screen at all; a group carries a dot while anything inside it is active. The groups adopt the engines' own buttons, moving the real nodes in with their ids and their listeners, so nothing exists twice and the old crowded bars empty themselves. Which actions combine is now stated and checked in a browser rather than assumed: cut and the radiographic styles read “+ they work together: the cut face is drawn in the style's own grey”, while an incompatible pair is disabled with its reason printed under it, not buried in a tooltip a phone never shows; and where one action simply takes precedence over another — a radiographic style pauses Q300's extra bone pass, because it sets the bone's brightness itself — that is said too, instead of disabling either. The model itself can now be drawn as a radiograph, a CT or an MRI, with one tap back to colour. Every number comes from the tissue table the pages already ship (33 classes, CT attenuation in Hounsfield units, MRI pools at 1.5 T, each value carrying its own verification status) and none is retyped in the viewer: the X‑ray is a true digitally reconstructed radiograph — alpha = 1 − e−µd, so ordinary alpha compositing over black accumulates Beer–Lambert's e−Σµd, order‑independently, with every closed shell crossed twice as a real beam crosses it — the CT is each tissue's own HU through a real window (MSK, soft tissue, bone), and the MRI is each tissue's own spin‑echo signal (T1, T2, PD fat‑sat). The right answers come out: cortical bone white and fat dark on CT, fat brightest on T1, free fluid brightest on T2, fat suppressed on PD fat‑sat. Measured in rendered pixels, not by eye: a style drops the picture's colour saturation from 28–57 to 0–2 and makes 100 % of its pixels pure grey, and the three styles sit tens of grey levels apart. What is convention rather than measurement is named in the ⓘ, including the path length per crossing (chosen by measuring where the contrast peaks) and the 15 of 33 classes whose CT value the data file itself marks unverified. All the page‑wide prose — licence, citations, conventions, the compatibility table — moved behind a small circled ⓘ at the side; the structure card keeps saying where its own geometry comes from. Plain fat is off when the page loads and one tap away, while still filling every cut face and cross‑section, because a section drawn without it reads as hollow. And beside the per‑system controls there is now one Depth control that peels the body inward in taps, with the layer name and number on the screen: skin → fat / superficial fascia → deep fascia → muscle → ligament → vessels and nerves → organs, leaving bone and cartilage. The fat step is the superficial fascia, by the structures' own Latin names — 17 of the 51 subcutaneous‑fat meshes are labelled Panniculus adiposus (tela subcutanea). What these pages do not carry is a separate membranous (Scarpa's) layer, and their own search data already said so; the ⓘ passes that on, and a test asserts the absence against the shipped pages rather than taking it on trust.
Since Q300 (1 Nov 2026) you can always get back out. The moment anything is hidden — an isolated structure, a tissue soloed, a preset that puts every tissue it does not name at zero, a side filter, a cut, a structure hidden from its own card — a chip appears at the bottom of the 3‑D view naming it, with its own × that undoes only that one, beside one Show everything button that ends them all; Esc does the same on a desktop, and every exit is a single Undo step. Nine separate ways of hiding something used to have nine exits in nine places, and nothing on screen said what was hidden. Beside the module reset there is now a reset of the model: pose, camera, visibility, isolation, opacity, marks, cuts, cross‑sections, slices, measurements and labels back to how the page loaded, in one tap, without leaving the module, undoable. And the skeleton is visible again in x‑ray and in fade: the bone is drawn once more over the soft tissue with the depth test off, so it no longer loses a multiplicative fight against however many translucent layers lie in front of it, with a three‑step Bone: Off · Clear · Strong control that appears only in those modes. Since Q298 (1 Nov 2026) the search on every viewer finds a structure by any name a clinician would use. The owner looked for the iliotibial tract as “ITB” and as “iliotibial band” and got nothing: each viewer was matching the query as one contiguous substring of one field, so an abbreviation could never match, a two-word query failed unless those words were next to each other in that order (“iliotibial band” missed “Iliotibial (IT) tract/band”, which contains both), the Latin name and the group were never searched at all, and one typo returned nothing. Now ITB, IT band, iliotibial band, tractus iliotibialis and הרצועה האיליוטיביאלית all reach it, and the row says which name matched — Iliotibial tract (ITB) — so you learn the canonical name. The layer is a real data file of published abbreviations, second names, Latin forms, Hebrew names and the eponyms this project cites, resolved against each viewer's own structure list at build time; ambiguous abbreviations (GM, TA) offer every expansion and name each one, and a name we cite but ship no mesh for (Colles’ and Scarpa’s fascia, Gerdy’s tubercle, the pectineal ligament of Cooper) says so instead of returning nothing. Marking several structures is now one action: Mark all N takes every match of a search in a single undo step, Range takes a run of rows, and On model turns on marking by tapping the body. And after you pick one structure, three one-tap expansions add to the marked set: Adjacent — measured on that viewer’s own meshes, every surface rasterised into a shared 2 mm voxel grid, neighbours within 3.46 mm (10,190 to 58,015 neighbour pairs per viewer); Related — the same muscle’s other heads, its tendon, bursae, nerve supply, the artery that supplies it, the bones it attaches to, the joints it crosses and its antagonists, from this project’s own cited data, with the four rule-based kinds labelled inferred; and Functional group — the named textbook groups, the groups derived from the cited action data, and extras such as the tensors of the iliotibial tract (tensor fasciae latae and gluteus maximus). Searching a group name (“hamstrings”, “rotator cuff”, “quads”, “calf”) offers the group itself as one chip.

Since Q296 (31 Oct 2026) the four Z-Anatomy viewers (Base and Combined) carry four inner-ear structures no model here had before: the bony labyrinth (cochlea, vestibule and the three semicircular canals as one cavity, 0.17-0.20 cm³), the osseous spiral lamina, the round window and the stapedius muscle, on both sides. They are real meshes imported from the SPL Inner Ear Atlas of the Open Anatomy Project (flat-panel CT of a human temporal bone, about 140 µm; Bartling, Jakab and Kikinis, German Cancer Research Center and the Surgical Planning Laboratory, Brigham and Women's Hospital; 3D Slicer License Part B, research use only), placed by registering that atlas's own malleus, incus and stapes onto each page's own three ossicles (residual 0.20 mm median, 0.57 mm p95). Each card says so, names the citation and the licence, and states that the mesh is a modified version of the atlas model (rescaled, decimated, and mirrored on the left, because the atlas ear is a right ear). The two imaging-based viewers are unchanged: neither her CT nor his cryosections resolve the ossicles, so there is nothing there to register onto.
Since Q295 (29 Oct 2026) every viewer has a Fat system: subcutaneous fat, deep fat (intermuscular, visceral / retroperitoneal, ischioanal, orbital, buccal, popliteal, axillary) and the remaining unlabelled soft tissue, 51 meshes per body, measured on the Visible Human female's CT (fat HU) and the male's colour cryosections in the space no other structure covers (the Base pages carry them by a registration onto the Z body). Faded in 3-D at load, it fills the cut face, so a cross-section is no longer mostly empty (void inside the skin 26-86 % before, 8-40 % after, per region).
Since Q294 (29 Oct 2026) every viewer has Undo / Redo (60 steps) and a Reset of the current module, a one-line summary of what is marked and how it is shown, slices of one part (a finger, the forearm) with thickness, interval and a display-only spread, full names behind abbreviations, and distance / angle / Cobb measuring on the model and the image panes; in the ultrasound, thin sheets keep their true thickness at every zoom, the probe is dragged on the skin with a rotate / tilt pad, each probe type has its own shape, and deltoid, pectoralis major, trapezius and rectus femoris have fibres per bundle.
Since Q293 (29 Oct 2026) every viewer has the reflected (indirect) head of rectus femoris, built by stated rules from cadaveric dimensions and badged rule-based; the Combined hands are re-articulated on their own bones (the phalanges were seated apart); and the Learn simulation moves every joint of a two-joint muscle in one phase (rectus femoris flexes the hip while it extends the knee).
