## 3. How the asymmetry is measured

For every matched pair the LEFT group is reflected about the model's midsagittal plane (winding reversed) and compared with the RIGHT group: volume ratio R/L (exact for closed meshes; for open / non-manifold meshes the voxel solid of the sealed surface, 'volume (voxel)'; area ratio only when the surface encloses nothing), centroid offset after mirroring (position / posture), median symmetric surface distance (msd) and 95th-percentile Hausdorff (hd95) from 2,000 area-weighted samples per side, then a rigid ICP of the mirrored left onto the right (translation, rotation, residual msd / hd95 after alignment) and the overlap IoU of the voxel solids (common grid, pitch about extent / 70, 0.8-3 mm), both right after the plane mirroring and after the rigid alignment. The flags use the aligned IoU (a posed limb is not a shape difference); the raw mirrored IoU and the centroid offset show the posture asymmetry (limbs lie differently in these bodies: offsets of 40-100 mm are posture). Skin and fascia are sheets: area ratio and a shell IoU (no solid, never IoU-flagged); structures thinner than 4 mm (mean thickness 2V/A: nerves, small vessels, fine ligaments) keep their IoU in the table but are not flagged by it (a 1 mm misalignment of a 2 mm tube is IoU 0.3, not a shape difference); their ratio is still flagged.
