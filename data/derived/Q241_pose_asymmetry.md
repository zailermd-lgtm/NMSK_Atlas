Generated by `scripts/anatomy/q241/run_asym.py` (+ `asym.py`, `rig.py`, `skeleton.py`, `skin.py`); machine-readable: `data/derived/Q241_pose_asymmetry.json`. Reads the CURRENT pages (build/q238 = hub v34), never edits them. Frame: mm, +X = the subject's right. Builds on Q237 (`Q237_pairing.json`: pairing, midsagittal planes, tissue metrics).

## 1. Method

* **Joint angles of the skeleton as built.** Landmarks are computed from each page's OWN bones (femoral / humeral head spheres, epicondylar axes, malleolar axis, sole plane from heel + metatarsal heads, ASIS / PSIS of the pelvis, radial vs ulnar styloid, finger / toe joint centres ...). The LEFT limb is reflected about the model's midsagittal plane first, so every limb is measured as a RIGHT limb and equal numbers mean the same anatomical posture. Shoulder / hip: flexion about the mediolateral axis, then abduction, then rotation (intrinsic order, relative to the trunk / pelvis frame). Elbow, knee: hinge angle. Forearm: AAOS convention (0 = neutral, + = supination). Wrist, fingers, ankle (talocrural) and subtalar angle as defined in `docs/pose_rom_table.md` / `rig.py`. Palmar direction from the forearm flexor vs extensor muscles of the same model. Difference = right - left.
* **Bone registration.** The mirrored LEFT bones of every segment are rigidly registered (trimmed ICP, 5 initialisations) onto the RIGHT bones: the world rotation / translation that would give the left segment the mirrored right pose (midline bones are registered onto themselves = rotation of the segment about its own symmetry plane).
* **Spine / pelvis / head.** The bilateral-symmetry plane of every vertebra, of the pelvis (hip bones + sacrum), the skull and the thorax is fitted (mirror chamfer, Nelder-Mead) and compared with the model plane: axial rotation (+ = turned to the subject's left) and lateral tilt (+ = right side up). Pelvic tilt / obliquity / rotation = pelvis frame (hip centres, ASIS / PSIS) against the trunk frame.
* **Tissue before / after pose normalisation.** For every paired structure of the Q237 table the mirrored left tissue is moved by the per-bone rigid registration of its segments (linear blend skinning with the weights of the pose rig, `skin.py`) and IoU / centroid offset / mean surface distance are recomputed against the right structure with the Q237 voxel code. `IoU mirror` = Q237 (mirroring only), `IoU pose` = after the bone-driven alignment, `IoU ICP` = Q237's per-structure ICP (an upper bound that uses the tissue itself, not the bones). Volume ratios are pose-invariant.

## 2. Thresholds (ASSUMPTIONS, stated, not from a single standard; literature check in Q245)

Resting left-right differences of healthy adults standing in a relaxed posture are a few degrees (pelvic obliquity and trunk lateral deviation below about 2-3 degrees, hip / knee / ankle angles within about 5 degrees; gait-symmetry literature, e.g. Sadeghi et al. 2000, Herzog et al. 1989; leg-length / pelvic obliquity: Knutson 2005). Used here: shoulder 5 deg, elbow 5 deg, forearm 8 deg, wrist 5 deg, fingers 8 deg, hip 5 deg, knee 5 deg, ankle 5 deg, toes 8 deg, pelvis 3 deg, spine 3 deg, head 5 deg; rotations about a long axis (shoulder / hip rotation, forearm, subtalar) tolerance x1.6 because landmarks define them less well; finger / toe groups are judged on the mean over the joints (landmark noise of tiny bones is about 4 deg on the Z-Anatomy bones). Tissue: IoU >= 0.6 and volume ratio R/L in 0.80-1.25 (the Q237 flags). **Verdict 'reasonable'** = no tolerance exceeded; otherwise 'needs fix' for the BASIC models (they must be symmetric fit targets) and 'posture-asymmetric specimen' for the reconstructed pages (their asymmetry is measured; never overwritten).

### 2.1 What the literature justifies (Q245; PubMed abstracts and the Vairo 2012 full text - see `docs/pose_rom_table.md` References for DOI / PMID and what was read)

The tolerances are applied to the posture of ONE specimen as built; the literature below gives the left-right (dominant vs non-dominant) difference of the MAXIMAL range of motion, as GROUP MEANS. A group-mean difference is a lower bound for the spread of individual differences (the SD of the individual difference was not available in any source read), so the literature can justify a tolerance only as 'about the size of the typical mean difference', never as a 95% limit. The numbers are unchanged by Q245, therefore the verdicts below were NOT recomputed.

| tolerance | evidence read in Q245 | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| limb joints 5 deg (shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, ankle) | Elbow: dominant vs non-dominant < 1 deg (Zwerus 2019, n = 352, abstract). Shoulder (Vairo 2012 full text, n = 622 cadets, passive): flexion 0.8, ER arm at side 1.8, ER at 90 deg abduction 4.1, IR 3.5; the authors state that no mean difference exceeded 5 deg. Women: dominant ER +4.7, IR -3.5 deg (Conte 2009). Ankle dorsiflexion: no side difference (Baumbach 2014, n = 20). Hip / knee / ankle of men 30-40 y: no significant side difference (Roaas & Andersson 1982, abstract). 90 women 18-59 y: 34 of 60 ROM differ significantly between sides, the largest mean difference is 7.5 deg (Macedo & Magee 2008, abstract). Right-hand-dominant men 18-22 y: right range smaller than left, significant (Gunal 1996, no values in the abstract). | **justified as the typical ceiling of the mean difference** for elbow, knee, ankle, hip flexion / abduction and shoulder flexion / abduction (all <= 5 deg); **marginal** for shoulder rotation and hip rotation (see next row); not a 95% limit |
| long-axis rotations 8 deg (= 5 x 1.6; also the forearm and finger / toe groups) | Rotation is where dominance acts: dominant ER gain / IR loss 3.5-4.7 deg in non-athletes (Vairo 2012, Conte 2009), 12 / 12 deg in collegiate softball players (Biaggi 2021), ER gain 1.8-8.8 and IR loss 3.0-15.3 deg in men's tennis (Myers 2024, systematic review); largest mean difference in any joint of non-athlete women 7.5 deg (Macedo & Magee 2008). Vairo 2012 quotes mean differences < 7 or < 8 deg as probably without clinical relevance and the AMA Guides as < 10 deg at the shoulder complex (second-hand, originals not read). Hip rotation: only 61% of 120 adults had a symmetric rotation balance (Kouyoumdjian 2012, abstract). | **8 deg justified for non-athletes** (covers the dominance effect and sits at Macedo's maximum); it would flag an overhead athlete (12 deg) - not a defect of the model. The 8 deg for forearm rotation, fingers and toes has **no supporting series with values** (Mallon 1991: no handedness effect in the fingers, no numbers) = assumption |
| wrist 5 deg | no side-to-side wrist series with values read (Allander 1974 abstract not available; Gunal 1996 wrist: right < left, no values). Kim 2014 wrist SD is 3.0-5.1 deg between subjects. | assumption |
| subtalar / ankle 5 deg x 1.6 | goniometric subtalar values are poorly reproducible between testers (inter-tester ICC 0.17-0.32 for inversion / eversion, Elveru 1988, abstract), so a landmark-based foot angle cannot be more precise than the clinical one | assumption, probably tight for the foot |
| pelvis 3 deg, spine 3 deg, head 5 deg | these are POSTURE tolerances, not ROM. Cervical / lumbar left-right ROM differs by <= 1 deg in the one series read (Apti 2023: lateral flexion 42.1 / 41.4, rotation 71.1 / 70.2, lumbar rotation 38.4 / 38.6 deg), which does not contradict 3 deg, but gives no posture limit. Herzog 1989 is a force-plate symmetry index (95% limits from +-4% to over +-13,000%), Sadeghi 2000 a review of gait symmetry and Knutson 2005 the leg-length difference (5.2 +- 4.1 mm): none gives a joint-angle tolerance. | assumption (the 'about 2-3 degrees' sentence above is NOT supported by the three citations as read in their abstracts) |
